Rank, exponent and the weighted Gao identity for finite abelian \(p\)-groups

Programs and outputs · Abílio Lemos and Victor G. L. Neumann

This page contains the programs used for the computations in Section 9 of the paper, and their outputs.

Throughout, \(G\) is a finite abelian \(p\)-group, \(A\) is the set of units modulo \(\exp(G)\), and \(s_A(G)\) is the smallest \(t\) such that every sequence over \(G\) of length \(t\) has an \(A\)-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length \(\exp(G)\). For an integer \(m\ge 2\), the searches compute the number \(\mu_m(G)\) defined in Section 9.1 of the paper: the largest length of a sequence over \(G\) without \(A\)-weighted zero-sum subsequence of length \(m\) is \(m-1+\mu_m(G)\). In particular, \(s_A(G)=\exp(G)+\mu_{\exp(G)}(G)\).

Files

  • busca_sA-19-11-26.c
    C program for the exhaustive searches of Section 9. Its header describes the method, the arguments and the runs quoted in the paper.
  • saidas_busca_sA-19-11-26.txt
    Outputs of the C program: each command and the line it printed.
  • verificacoes_artigo-19-11-26b.sage
    SageMath script: a second implementation of the search, and the checks of Section 9.3.
  • saidas_verificacoes_artigo-19-11-26b.txt
    Outputs of the SageMath script: the level medium, and the enumeration over \(\mathbb{Z}_4^3\oplus\mathbb{Z}_2\), which was run separately. The first lines give the version of Sage that was used.

How to run

The C program

cc -O2 -o busca_sA busca_sA-19-11-26.c
./busca_sA p m mode r m_1 ... m_r [a_1 ... a_r [b_1 ... b_r]]

Here \(G=\mathbb{Z}_{m_1}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathbb{Z}_{m_r}\) is a \(p\)-group with \(|G|\le 256\), and m is the length of the forbidden zero-sums (m = 0 stands for \(m=\exp(G)\)). With mode = 0 the search runs through all zero-free sequences without zero-sum subsequence of length \(m\), up to units. The modes 1, 2 and 3 restrict the search to sequences containing terms of prescribed orbits, given by the elements a and b; they are described in the header of the program. For example,

./busca_sA 3 0 0 3 9 3 3

prints

G = Z_9 + Z_3 + Z_3 : mode 0, nonzero orbits 22, sequences visited 7745097, m = 9, max(|T| - L_m(T)) = 4, m + max = 13 (exp + Omega = 13); sequences attaining the maximum: 2826, of which with L_m(T) = 0: 2826

that is, \(\mu_9(\mathbb{Z}_9\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3^2)=4\) and \(s_A(\mathbb{Z}_9\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3^2)=13\). On one core of an Intel Xeon processor at 2.1 GHz this search takes a few seconds, and the two large searches of Proposition 9.1 take about ten and eight minutes.

The SageMath script

sage verificacoes_artigo-19-11-26b.sage
sage verificacoes_artigo-19-11-26b.sage medium
sage verificacoes_artigo-19-11-26b.sage long

Without argument the script runs the quick checks. The level medium adds the searches for \(\mathbb{Z}_9\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3^2\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_3^4\), and the enumeration of the zero-sum free sequences over \(\mathbb{Z}_9\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3^3\), \(\mathbb{Z}_{16}\oplus\mathbb{Z}_2^3\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_8\oplus\mathbb{Z}_4\oplus\mathbb{Z}_2^2\). The level long adds the two large searches of Proposition 9.1 and the enumeration over \(\mathbb{Z}_4^3\oplus\mathbb{Z}_2\). The script uses only Python integers, lists, sets and dictionaries, and it decides the existence of zero-sums from the definition.

On one core of an Intel Xeon processor at 2.1 GHz, the level medium takes about 19 minutes, and the enumeration over \(\mathbb{Z}_4^3\oplus\mathbb{Z}_2\) about 32 minutes.

Checks of the SageMath script

  • (1) The numbers \(\mu_m(G)\): the searches of the C program, with the same numbers. The two large searches of Proposition 9.1 are run only at the level long.
  • (1') The shape of the sequences attaining \(\mu_p\) in \(\mathbb{Z}_3^2\), \(\mathbb{Z}_5^2\), \(\mathbb{Z}_7^2\), \(\mathbb{Z}_5^3\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_3^3\) (Section 9.3).
  • (2) Theorem 1.4: enumeration of the zero-sum free sequences over the groups listed in Section 9.3, and the test of whether each of them is a chain sequence.
  • (3) Proposition 4.10: the sequence is zero-sum free, checked from the definition for \(p=2\), \(3\) and \(5\).
  • (4) Lemma 6.1, checked exhaustively for the parameters listed in Section 9.3.
  • (5) Example 9.2: the sequence \((0,1)(1,0)(1,p)(p,1)\) over \(\mathbb{Z}_{p^2}^2\) is a minimal zero-sum sequence, for \(p=3\), \(5\) and \(7\); the numbers of minimal zero-sum sequences over \(\mathbb{Z}_9\oplus\mathbb{Z}_3\) and \(\mathbb{Z}_9^2\), by length.
